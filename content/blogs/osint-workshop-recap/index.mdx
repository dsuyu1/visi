We spent the evening on open-source intelligence — the research side of security, where everything you use is already public and the skill is in connecting it.

## Ground rules first

We opened with the rules, not the tools. Everything in the exercise targeted accounts and domains we set up ourselves. Doing this against a real person without permission is harassment, and against a company without a contract it is reconnaissance for an attack you are not authorized to run.

## What the exercise looked like

Teams got a fake company with a website, a few employee profiles, and some job postings, then built a profile of it from public sources alone. By the end most teams had the tech stack, a rough org chart, and a naming convention for email addresses.

The job postings gave up the most. A listing asking for experience with a specific EDR product tells an attacker exactly what they are up against.

## Defending against it

The last twenty minutes were about the other side: what your own organization leaks, and how much of it you can reasonably clean up. Usually not much — but knowing what is out there changes how you write a phishing simulation.
